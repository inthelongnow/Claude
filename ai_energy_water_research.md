# AI Energy and Water Usage: Current Status (March 2026)

## Executive Summary

The rapid expansion of AI infrastructure is driving unprecedented growth in energy and water consumption. Data centers are projected to consume over 1,000 TWh of electricity globally by 2026–2027, and AI workloads are expected to surpass non-AI workloads for the first time in 2026. Water consumption for cooling is equally concerning, with projections showing an 870% increase as new facilities come online.

---

## 1. Energy Consumption

### Global Scale
- Global data center electricity consumption reached ~415 TWh in 2024, accounting for approximately 1.5% of total global electricity consumption.
- Projected to exceed **1,000 TWh by 2026–2027**, which would rank data centers as the 5th largest electricity consumer globally (between Japan and Russia).
- The IEA projects data center consumption could reach ~945 TWh by 2030 in its Base Case scenario.
- Data center electricity consumption is growing at ~15% per year — more than 4x faster than all other sectors combined.

### AI as the Key Driver
- AI-specific (accelerated server) workloads are growing at **30% annually**, versus 9% for conventional server workloads.
- AI currently accounts for 5–15% of data center power use, projected to reach **35–50% by 2030**.
- In 2026, AI workloads (44 GW) are projected to surpass non-AI workloads (38 GW) for the first time.
- AI is forecast to use over half of all data center electricity by 2028.

### US-Specific Data
- US data centers now consume ~4.4% of national electricity (up from 1.9% in 2018), potentially reaching 12% by 2028.
- US data center demand projected to rise to 75.8 GW in 2026, 108 GW in 2028, and 134.4 GW in 2030.
- Data centers will drive **60% of overall US electrical load increase** through 2030 — more than any other sector, including electric vehicles.
- A single hyperscale AI data center can use as much power as 100,000 to 2 million homes.
- US data center energy demand expected to nearly double from 80 GW to 150 GW between 2025 and 2028.

### Regional Hotspots
- **Virginia:** Data centers consumed 26% of all state electricity in 2023.
- **Ireland:** 21% of national electricity in 2022 was attributable to data centers, forecast to reach 32% by 2026.
- **China and US** account for nearly 80% of global data center electricity consumption growth to 2030.

### Investment
- Companies worldwide expected to invest nearly **$7 trillion** in building and upgrading data centers through 2030.
- Goldman Sachs estimates ~$720 billion needed for grid upgrades through 2030.
- PJM projects a 6 GW shortfall by 2027 (equivalent to six large nuclear power plants).

## 2. Water Consumption

### Per-Query Impact
- UC Riverside researchers estimate each 100-word AI prompt consumes roughly **one bottle of water (~519 mL)** for cooling.
- Training GPT-3 in Microsoft's US data centers alone evaporated 700,000 liters of freshwater.

### Facility-Level Consumption
- A medium-sized data center consumes up to ~110 million gallons of water per year (equivalent to ~1,000 households).
- Large data centers use up to **5 million gallons per day** — equivalent to a city of 50,000 people.
- Google reported 5.6 billion gallons of water consumption in 2023, a 24% year-over-year increase.

### Projections
- Texas data centers projected to use 49 billion gallons in 2025, rising to **399 billion gallons by 2030**.
- US data centers consumed an estimated 21.2 billion liters in 2014 and 66 billion liters in 2023.
- By 2028, data centers could collectively use as much water as **18.5 million households**.
- Cooling water demand may increase by **870%** as new facilities come online.

### Why Water Is Critical
- Water absorbs heat **3,000x more effectively** than air, making it far better suited for cooling.
- Cooling accounts for 30–40% of total data center energy use.
- ~80% of water withdrawn by data centers evaporates (the remainder is discharged to wastewater facilities).
- Average rack density expected to grow from 36 kW (2023) to 50 kW (2027), requiring more cooling.

### Water Stress Risks
- An assessment of 9,055 facilities indicates that by the 2050s, nearly **45% may face high water stress exposure**.
- The AI water footprint could reach 312.5–764.6 billion liters in 2025.

---

## 3. Carbon Footprint

- AI systems' carbon footprint estimated at **32.6–79.7 million tons of CO₂** in 2025 — comparable to New York City's total emissions.
- Over **60% of US data center power** still comes from fossil fuels.
- Renewables currently supply 27% of data center electricity, projected to grow 22% annually.
- Renewables may meet nearly half of anticipated data center electricity demand growth through 2030.

---

## 4. The Energy-Water Trade-off

A fundamental tension exists in data center sustainability:
- **Water-based cooling** is more energy-efficient but increases water consumption.
- **Air-based cooling** conserves water but requires significantly more electricity.
- This creates a zero-sum dynamic where optimizing for one resource often comes at the expense of the other.

---

## 5. Transparency and Policy Concerns

- Data center operators frequently withhold information about water usage, energy usage, air quality impacts, and emissions.
- The water footprint of AI is even more difficult to assess than its carbon footprint due to the complexity of direct and indirect water use.
- Between March and June 2025, community opposition led to **$98 billion in data center projects being blocked or delayed**.
- Further disclosures from operators are urgently needed to improve accuracy of environmental impact estimates.

---

## 6. Mitigation Efforts

- **Microsoft** launched a new zero-water-cooling data center design in 2025.
- **Closed-loop cooling systems** can reduce freshwater use by up to 70%.
- Renewable energy procurement by tech companies continues to grow, though it has not kept pace with demand increases.
- Industry investment in alternative cooling technologies (liquid cooling, immersion cooling) is accelerating.
