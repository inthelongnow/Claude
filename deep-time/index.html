Chapter I · The Cosmos 13.8 billion years ago

Everything, everywhere, at once.

The observable universe is smaller than an atom and hotter than anything since. In its first fraction of a second it swells by a factor of at least 1026.

380,000 years later

Light breaks free.

The fog of plasma clears and light travels freely for the first time. That glow still reaches us from every direction, cooled to 2.725 K: the cosmic microwave background.

~200 million years later

The first stars.

Gravity pulls the dark into knots. Stars hundreds of times the Sun’s mass ignite, burn out within a few million years, and scatter carbon, oxygen and iron.

4.6 billion years ago

A star is born from others’ ashes.

In a quiet arm of the Milky Way, a collapsing cloud of recycled stardust becomes the Sun. Press and hold anywhere to set off the supernova that may have triggered it.

Chapter II · Hadean Eon

A world of molten rock

Drag sideways to tilt the magma ocean

4.54 billion years ago

Earth assembles from rubble. Heat from collisions and radioactive decay keeps the whole surface molten.

~4.5 billion years ago

A Mars-sized world, Theia, strikes the young Earth. The debris forms the Moon, which then orbits so close it looks enormous. It is still drifting away, 3.8 cm a year.

4.4 billion years ago

Zircon crystals from Jack Hills, Western Australia, the oldest pieces of Earth yet found, suggest liquid water already exists.

~4.0 billion years ago

Steam rains out for millions of years. The crust hardens and the oceans fill. The stage is set.

Chapter VII · The next 10,000 years

The Long Now

“It ticks once a year, bongs once a century, and the cuckoo comes out every millennium.”Danny Hillis, on the 10,000-year clock being built inside a Texas mountain

Drag clockwise around the dial · 3 turns

Tick. One year has passed.

Today’s melody was sequence #1 of 3,628,800, the number of ways to order ten bells: enough for a new one every day for almost 10,000 years.

The century bong is 73 years away. The millennium cuckoo, 973.

Then, silence.
