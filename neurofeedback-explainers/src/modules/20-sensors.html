The signal

Where the sensors go, and why stillness helps

Sensor positions follow a worldwide map of the head. The brain's signal is tiny, so a blink or a tight jaw can drown it out.

Reading the names

Letter
The region underneath: F frontal, C central, T temporal, P parietal, O occipital. Fp is the very front.
Number
Odd numbers are on the left, even numbers on the right. A z means the midline.
Spacing
Positions are measured as 10% and 20% steps between landmarks on your own head, so the map fits every head size. It is called the 10-20 system.

T3, T4, T5 and T6 are written T7, T8, P7 and P8 in the newer naming.

Brain signal, and everything else

Try
    • The signal is tinyBrain activity at the scalp is measured in millionths of a volt. Eyes, muscles and movement produce signals many times larger.
    • Software cannot always tellLeft alone, a blink can read as slow brain activity and a tight jaw as fast activity. That would reward the wrong thing.
    • So we keep it cleanFeedback pauses during blinks and movement, I check the signal during set-up, and for a brain map the noisy stretches are removed before analysis.
