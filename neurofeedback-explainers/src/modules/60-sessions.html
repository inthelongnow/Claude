The training

What your sessions will be like

Each visit is about an hour, with 30 to 45 minutes of actual training. Two or three visits a week works best when your schedule allows.

The hour

Check-inSet-upTraining · 30 to 45 minutesWrap-up
  1. Check-inHow you have been since last time: sleep, mood, energy, focus, anything new. We update your symptom tracking.
  2. Set-upSensors go on with a little paste. I check the signal and sort out any trouble spots before we start.
  3. TrainingYou sit comfortably and watch and listen while the feedback runs. I stay with you, watch the signal, and adjust as needed.
  4. Wrap-upSensors off and clean-up, how the session felt, anything to look out for, and scheduling.

From first meeting onward

  1. Assessment interviewYour history, current concerns, sleep, health, medications, and what you hope will change.
  2. Brain map, if it fitsOffered when it is appropriate and you want one. Some people are not ready for one yet or prefer not to have one, and we can plan without it.
  3. Regular trainingAbout an hour a visit, ideally two or three times a week, with your reports steering each session.
  4. Review as we goWe look at your tracking together at regular points and decide whether to continue, change approach, space sessions out, or stop.

Planning a block

Sessions
A week

For planning only. Studies have typically used about 15 to 40 sessions. Your number depends on your goals and how you respond, and we review it together as we go.

Evidence on spacing is limited. One trial in athletes, and a pooled analysis of ADHD studies, favoured three sessions a week over fewer.

What you might notice

Most reactions are mild and pass within a day: feeling tired, wired, headachy, spacey or more emotional than usual, or a change in sleep or dreams. More vivid dreams are one that clients often mention, though that comes from clinical observation and has not been tested in trials. Some people notice nothing at first.

In large trials no serious harm from training has been reported, and passing reactions like these are common even in comparison groups given sham feedback.

Tell me about all of it, pleasant or not. A reaction is information. It tells me how to adjust the training, and the adjustment usually settles it.

Your part

  • Notice and report. What changes during and between sessions, for better or worse, is the main thing that guides the training.
  • Keep taking medication as prescribed. Any change is for you and your prescriber to decide. Please tell me when one happens, so I can read your responses correctly.
  • Keep up everything else that helps. Therapy, exercise, sleep routines and other treatments are all welcome alongside this.
  • Come as you are. If you slept badly or the day has been hard, say so. It shapes the session and nothing is wasted.
