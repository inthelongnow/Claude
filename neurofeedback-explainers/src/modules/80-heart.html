Heart and breath

Your heart does not tick like a clock. It speeds up a little as you breathe in and slows as you breathe out. Slow, gentle breathing makes that swing larger, and practising it is called heart-rate variability biofeedback.

Finding your pace

Most adults get their largest swing somewhere between 4.5 and 6.5 breaths a minute. Stay at each pace for a minute or two and compare. Each pace you try is logged here.

Near enough is good enough: in a four-week trial, training at a personal pace and at a fixed 6 a minute worked about equally well.
Breathe gently. Slower, not bigger. If you feel light-headed, tingly or short of air, you are over-breathing: take smaller, softer breaths, or go back to breathing normally.
A small swing is not a bad sign. The swing naturally gets smaller with age and varies with fitness and some medications. In one study, healthy but inactive men in their sixties swung about 10 beats a minute during slow breathing, compared with about 22 in young men. What matters is how your own pattern changes with practice.
What practice does. Across 58 randomized trials, this kind of training showed small-to-moderate benefits, largest for anxiety, low mood, anger and performance. Programmes usually ask for short daily practice, often under 20 minutes. Benefits are clearer in people with symptoms than in healthy people, and not everyone responds in the same way.

For learning only. This is not a medical device and cannot detect heart problems. If your rhythm looks irregular, or you have a heart or breathing condition, talk with your physician before practising.
