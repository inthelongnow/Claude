# SciSpace prompts for the remaining evidence gaps

Paste each of these into SciSpace (Tools menu, Deep Research or Verified Report). They are written so the output can be dropped straight into the guide: every study gets the same fields, every claim carries its design label, and nothing comes from clinic websites.

Run them in this order. The first three matter most, because they cover the parts of your practice where published evidence is thinnest.

## Shared instructions (already included in each prompt)

- Peer-reviewed human studies only. Mark preprints, conference abstracts, theses and book chapters as such. Do not use clinic websites, blogs, vendor material or press releases.
- Label the design of every study: sham-controlled RCT, RCT against active treatment, RCT against waiting list or usual care, non-randomised controlled, cohort, case series, case report.
- Extract the same fields for every study (listed in each prompt).
- Report null and negative results as carefully as positive ones.
- State who funded the study and whether any author sells equipment or training for the method.

## 1. Infra-low frequency neurofeedback

```
Review the published evidence on infra-low frequency (ILF) neurofeedback, including the Othmer method (Cygnet) and infra-slow fluctuation (ISF) training. Keep these two approaches separate in the results and say which one each study used.

Include every design: randomised trials, non-randomised controlled studies, cohorts, case series and case reports. Label the design of each study. Peer-reviewed human studies only; mark preprints, conference abstracts, theses and book chapters as such. Do not use clinic websites, vendor material or blogs.

For each study extract: (1) design and control condition (sham feedback, active treatment, waiting list or usual care, none); (2) sample size, age range and condition; (3) protocol: electrode sites, how the training frequency was chosen, number of sessions, session length, sessions per week; (4) primary outcome and result with numbers, and whether the difference from control was statistically significant; (5) whether outcome raters were blinded; (6) adverse events and how they were assessed; (7) follow-up length; (8) funding and conflicts of interest; (9) one plain sentence a client could understand.

Then answer: For which conditions is there any controlled evidence, and what did it show? Which controlled studies found no difference? What in-session and after-session reactions are described, and how often? What do the developers say about how the training frequency is optimised and which sites are used first? Finish with what a careful clinician can and cannot say to an adult client about the evidence for ILF.
```

## 2. Synchrony training

```
Review the published evidence on EEG synchrony, coherence or phase-synchrony neurofeedback, in which two or more scalp sites are rewarded for rising and falling together. Cover alpha synchrony (around 10 Hz), gamma synchrony (around 40 Hz), SMR coherence, and infra-low synchrony (around 0.01 to 0.05 Hz), and say clearly if nothing has been published on one of these.

This literature is thin, so include cohort studies, case series and case reports as well as trials, and label the design of every study. Peer-reviewed human studies only; mark preprints, conference abstracts, theses and book chapters as such. Do not use clinic websites, vendor material or blogs.

For each study extract: (1) design and control condition; (2) sample size, age range and condition; (3) protocol: sites, frequency, what exactly was rewarded (coherence, phase synchrony, summed amplitude), eyes open or closed, number and length of sessions, sessions per week; (4) whether the trained synchrony measure actually changed, and whether it changed more than in the control; (5) clinical or cognitive outcome with numbers; (6) adverse events; (7) follow-up; (8) funding and conflicts of interest; (9) one plain sentence a client could understand.

Then answer: Is there evidence that synchrony can be trained? Is there any controlled evidence that training it changes symptoms? What is published on 0.01 Hz, 10 Hz and 40 Hz two-channel synchrony as used in the Othmer method? Finish with what a careful clinician can and cannot say to an adult client.
```

## 3. Children and teenagers

```
Review the published evidence on EEG neurofeedback in children and adolescents (under 18), for any condition: ADHD, anxiety, autism, tic disorders, trauma, sleep, headache, learning difficulties and epilepsy.

Peer-reviewed human studies only; mark preprints, conference abstracts, theses and book chapters as such. Do not use clinic websites, vendor material or blogs. Label the design of every study.

For each study extract: (1) design and control condition; (2) sample size and age range; (3) condition; (4) protocol and dose: type of neurofeedback, sites, number and length of sessions, sessions per week; (5) primary outcome and result with numbers, and who rated it (parent, teacher, blinded clinician, objective test); (6) adverse events, how they were assessed, and when in the course they occurred; (7) drop-out rate and reasons; (8) follow-up length; (9) one plain sentence a parent could understand.

Then answer: What is known about safety and tolerability in children, including the youngest ages studied? Do results differ between parent ratings and blinded ratings? Is there evidence on session length, session frequency or total dose for children? What is known for conditions other than ADHD? Is anything published on Alpha-Theta or deep-state training in minors, and what cautions are given? Finish with what a careful clinician can and cannot say to a parent.
```

## 4. Depression in adults

```
Review the published evidence on EEG neurofeedback for depression in adults. Keep EEG neurofeedback separate from fMRI neurofeedback and from heart-rate variability biofeedback, and report each separately.

Peer-reviewed human studies only; mark preprints, conference abstracts, theses and book chapters as such. Do not use clinic websites, vendor material or blogs. Label the design of every study.

For each study extract: (1) design and control condition (sham feedback, active treatment, waiting list or usual care, none); (2) sample size, age range, diagnosis and whether participants were on antidepressants; (3) protocol: alpha asymmetry, high-beta down-training, SMR, ILF, Alpha-Theta or other; sites; number and length of sessions; sessions per week; (4) primary outcome and result with numbers; whether raters were blinded; (5) remission and response rates if given; (6) whether the trained EEG measure changed; (7) adverse events; (8) follow-up; (9) one plain sentence a client could understand.

Then answer: What do sham-controlled trials show compared with uncontrolled ones? Does frontal alpha asymmetry hold up as a marker or a training target? Is there evidence for neurofeedback as an add-on to medication or psychotherapy? Finish with what a careful clinician can and cannot say to an adult client.
```

## 5. Attention and ADHD in adults

```
Review the published evidence on EEG neurofeedback for attention problems and ADHD in adults (18 and over). Report child and adolescent studies only where they are the basis for an adult protocol, and keep them in a separate table.

Peer-reviewed human studies only; mark preprints, conference abstracts, theses and book chapters as such. Do not use clinic websites, vendor material or blogs. Label the design of every study.

For each study extract: (1) design and control condition; (2) sample size, age range, and whether participants were on stimulant medication; (3) protocol: theta/beta, SMR, slow cortical potentials, ILF, z-score or other; sites; number and length of sessions; sessions per week; (4) primary outcome and result with numbers; whether ratings were blinded; (5) whether participants learned to change the trained EEG measure, and whether learners did better; (6) adverse events; (7) follow-up length and whether gains held; (8) one plain sentence a client could understand.

Then answer: What do blinded and sham-controlled adult trials show compared with open-label and personalised studies? Is there evidence on session frequency or total number of sessions? What is known about combining neurofeedback with medication? What do supporters and critics of the sham-controlled trials each argue? Finish with what a careful clinician can and cannot say to an adult client.
```

## After each run

1. Export the review (PDF or Markdown) and the extraction table (CSV).
2. Upload both to the Claude session that maintains the guide. The table can be checked against the guide's "What research says" section row by row.
3. Before any wording reaches clients, run SciSpace's Citation Audit Agent on the review, and treat a GRADE rating from SciSpace as a draft for your own judgement.
